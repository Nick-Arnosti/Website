Is the ‘Mississippi Miracle’ Credible?

Nick Arnosti

2025/12/20

 

Background: “Mississippi Miracle”

Over the past 20 years, Mississippi has seen its ranks on 4th and 8th grade tests rise (both before and after adjusting for demographics).

Mention partly due to decline elsewhere.

Questioning the Analysis

I follow Andrew Gelman’s blog, and have

The analysis in their paper is simply not serious.

  • Some of the aggregage statistics they report are just flat out wrong (which they have subsequently acknowledged).
  • Their analysis involves no real data.
  • Their analysis essentially assumes that students who are held back from fourth grade disappear from the data entirely (rather than appearing in the subsequent year of data).

Now, I often rely on toy examples to make important points clearly. Here, the point they are making is that dropping the lowest decile of any measure will raise the average of the remaining observations. An analogy they make is that if you hold back the shortest kids in third grade, don’t be surprised if the average height in fourth grade rises.
