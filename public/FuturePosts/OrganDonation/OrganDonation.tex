\section{$V$ Realized with Outcomes}

Assume: $V$ and $c$ are independent, cost paid by all people listed (not just those who actually donate).

Death rate $\beta$; number of organs per death $\alpha$; rate of need $\theta$.

Assume undersupply (overdemand): $\alpha \beta < \theta$. This implies that under both donor and donor + value, only donors will receive organs.


\subsection{Donor Only}

I donate at cost $c$, and benefit with probability $\theta \left( \frac{\alpha \beta}{\theta} \right)$, with average benefit $E[V] = \int_0^\infty \overline{G}(v) dv$. Threshold will be $c_{d}^* = \alpha \beta E[V]$. 

Welfare gain from transplantation is number of transplants times average benefit of a transplant, which is $\alpha \beta E[V] F(\alpha \beta E[V])$. {\color{red} Comes at donation cost...} %$\int_{-\infty}^{c_d^*} F(c) dc$.

Cost to marginal donor, $c^*$, is smaller than benefit to average recipient, $E[V]$.


\subsection{Donor + Value}

Define $v^*$ to be solution to $\overline{G}(v^*) = \alpha \beta/\theta$. 

I donate at cost $c$, and benefit with probability $\theta \overline{G}(v^*) = \theta \left( \frac{\alpha \beta}{\theta} \right)$, with average benefit $E[v \vert v \geq v^*] = v^* + \frac{1}{\overline{G}(v^*)} \int_{v^*}^\infty \overline{G}(v) dv$. Threshold will be $c_{dv}^* = \theta \left(v^* \overline{G}(v^*) + \int_{v^*}^\infty \overline{G}(v) dv \right)$, which can be shown to be higher than before, so more donate.

Welfare is number of transplants, $\alpha \beta F(c_{dv}^*)$, times benefit per transplant $E[v \vert v \geq v^*]$.  {\color{red} Comes at donation cost...}%$\int_{-\infty}^{c_{dv}^*} F(c) dc$.
