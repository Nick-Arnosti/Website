Conjecture 1 Given a reserve graph G, there exist a set of upper quotas \(Q\) such that the feasible sets associated with \(Q\) are exactly \(F^{hard}(G)\).

Proof Sketch. For each set of applicants \(S \subseteq A\), define a quota category to be \(S\), and define the associated upper quota to be \(|N(S)|\).

Suppose we have a feasible selection \(S\). This means that there is a matching of \(G\) that matches everyone in \(S\). But any matching in \(G\) must satisfy the constraints above.

Conversely, suppose we have a selection \(S\) that complies with all quotas. We must show that there is a matching of \(G\) that matches all of \(S\).

Something interesting about this is that hard reserves always induce a matroid, so this quota structure must also induce a matroid (even though categories are not nested).

The following example shows that we can’t do the reverse: reserves are less expressive than quotas.

Example 1 There are three disjoint traits, and an upper quota of 1 on each, as well as a global upper quota of 2 on all applicants. There are two applicants with each of the disjoint traits. Then we can take any of the \(6\) choose \(2\) = 15 pairs of applicants, except the three pairs where the applicants share a trait. This cannot be replicated with two reserved positions.

Example 2. There is a lower quota of 1 on traits \(A\) and \(B\), and a gloabl upper quota of 2. Applicant 0 has neither trait, applicant 1 has trait \(A\), applicant 2 has trait \(B\), and applicant 3 has traits \(A\) and \(B\). Then the feasible selections are \(\{0,3\}, \{1,2\}, \{1,3\}, \{2,3\}\). This cannot be replicated with two reserved positions: because \(\{1,2\}\) is feasible, we must have that 1 is eligible for \(p_1\) and 2 is eligible for \(p_2\). Because \(\{0,3\}\) is feasible, we must have that \(0\) is eligible for some trait. Thus, either \(\{0,1\}\) or \(\{0,2\}\) must be feasible.

Conjecture 2 Suppose that (i) quota categories form a hierarchy, and (ii) the only upper quota is a global quota. Then there exists a reserve graph G such that the feasible sets under quotas are exactly the same as the \(F^{hard}(G)\).

This is FALSE. \(F^{hard}(G)\) includes the empty set, which may not be feasible. I think I mean that the maximal elements of the feasible sets are equal (or equivalently, the downward closure of the feasible sets).

This is still not true, as the feasible sets under quotas may be empty, while for reserves it will not be. Perhaps I want to assume that there is a feasible selection?

Other Thoughts.

What guarantees do reserves give you? For a set of applicants \(A\), if you have reserved \(R_A\) positions for this set, your final selection \(S\) will satisfy \(|S \cap A| \ge \min(R_A,|A|)\).

Precedence order effects: * With minimum quotas, want algorithm to take you early * With reserves, want algorithm to take you late
