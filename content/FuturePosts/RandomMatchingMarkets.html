--- title: 'Predicting Outcomes in Random Matching Markets' date: '2024-11-08' author: Nick Arnosti tags: School Choice draft: true ---

Suppose you have \(N_{students}\) students and \(N_{schools}\) schools.

Each school has capacity \(C\), and each student lists \(\ell\) schools in a uniformly random order.

This model was first studied by Pittel, in the case where \(N_{students} = N_{schools} = \ell\). In that case, there is a large core. Then Roth and Peranson proposed \(N_{students} = N_{schools} > \ell\). More recently, Ashlagi Kanoria and Leshno analyze the case \(m \ne n = \ell\), and found something surprising.

This post is on a surprisingly simple and accurate approximation to these outcomes. This comes from my paper “Lottery Design for School Choice.”

\[\begin{equation} N_{students}(1-(1-P(\lambda))^\ell) = N_{schools} \mathcal{E}(\lambda).\label(balance) \end{equation}\] where \[\begin{align} \mathcal{E}(\lambda) & = 1 - e^{-\lambda}\\ \mathcal{P}(\lambda) & = \mathcal{E}(\lambda)/\lambda \end{align}\]

Then the fraction of students who get their \(k^{th}\) choice is \[\begin{equation}F_k = P(\lambda) (1-P(\lambda))^{k-1}. \end{equation}\]

What insights do we get out of this? Can I make it simple?

Now, suppose students list different numbers of schools: student list lengths are drawn iid from some distribution \(\mathcal{D}\). Then @(eq:balance) becomes

\[\begin{equation} N_{students} \color{red}{\mathbb{E}_{\ell \sim \mathcal{D}}[}1-(1-P(\lambda))^\ell\color{red}{]} = N_{schools} \mathcal{E}(\lambda).\end{equation}\]

Meanwhile the fraction of students who list at least \(k\) schools and get their \(k^{th}\) choice is \[\begin{equation}F_k = \color{red}{\mathbb{P}_{\ell \sim \mathcal{D}}(\ell \ge k)} P(\lambda) (1-P(\lambda))^{k-1}. \end{equation}\]

Now suppose we want schools to have more than one seat. Say, they each have \(C_h\) seats. Then we simply re-define the enrollment function

\[\begin{equation} \mathcal{E}(\lambda) = \color{red}{\mathbb{E}[\min(Pois(\lambda),C)]} \end{equation}\]

Now suppose we want schools to have different capacities. Potentially, they are not all equally popular, either: suppose that each school \(h\) has a “weight” \(w_h\) which represents its popularity relative to the average school, so \(\sum_h w_h = N_{schools}\). (This popularity model was used by Immorlica Mahdian, Kojima Pathak, some Ashlagi papers and more).

\[\begin{equation} \mathcal{E}(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{N_{schools}}\sum_{h} \color{red}{w_h}\min(Pois(\color{red}{w_h}\lambda),C_\color{red}{h})] \end{equation}\]

The uniform case corresponds to taking \(w_h = 1\) for all \(h\).

In my paper, I show that these approximations are actually valid in the limit as \(\mathcal{D}\) is fixed and \(N_{students}\) and \(N_{schools}\) grow in a fixed ratio.

I also give analogous expressions for the case where school priorities are identical (rather than uniformly random). I use these expressions to show various insights (give examples – reproduce Ashlagi Kanoria Leshno, or Marx Schummer?). Check it out!
